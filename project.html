The business question

A fictional retailer sells Electronics, Fashion, Beauty and Home products in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and Kano. Which categories generate the most revenue, which earn better gross margins, and which have higher return rates?

This is a guided learning project created with assistance. The Excel and SQL analysis are supplied. The Power BI report still needs to be built and checked. All figures describe a simulated dataset.

Start with the source

The original file has 1,544 rows. Each business record represents one transaction for one product; quantity can exceed one. There are no multi-line orders or customer identifiers.

City, channel and status labels are trimmed and standardised. The original rows and the rejected-record export remain available for review.

Define the measures

Revenue and profit

For completed transactions, net revenue is quantity × unit price × (1 − discount). Cost of goods is quantity × unit cost. Gross profit is net revenue less cost of goods.

Returned transactions are fully refunded and the stock is assumed saleable at full cost, so their net revenue and cost of goods are both zero. This does not model damaged stock or return handling costs.

Rates and order value

Gross margin is total gross profit ÷ total net revenue. Return rate is returned transactions ÷ all clean transactions. Average completed order value is net revenue ÷ completed transactions.

Tax, shipping, overhead and inflation adjustments are excluded. The results measure gross profit, not net profit.

Findings in the sample

₦37.18m

The 1,500 clean transactions generate ₦37,176,500 net revenue and ₦13,729,300 gross profit. Overall gross margin is 36.9%.

Electronics contributes ₦13,014,050, the largest category revenue. Its 34.8% gross margin is lower than Beauty's margin.

45.1%

Beauty gross margin

Beauty produces the highest category gross margin, with ₦2,337,850 gross profit on ₦5,187,550 net revenue. Higher margin does not mean the largest total profit.

14.8%

Fashion return rate

62 of 418 Fashion transactions are returned, compared with an 8.2% overall return rate. The data has no return reasons, so it cannot explain the cause.

Inspect the SQL

The supplied scripts use SQLite. They create the raw table, prepare clean views, and answer eight analysis questions, including monthly growth with LAG.

SELECT category,
       SUM(net_revenue_ngn) AS net_revenue_ngn,
       SUM(gross_profit_ngn) AS gross_profit_ngn,
       SUM(gross_profit_ngn)
         / NULLIF(SUM(net_revenue_ngn), 0) AS gross_margin
FROM clean_sales
GROUP BY category
ORDER BY net_revenue_ngn DESC;

SQL outputs are reconciled against independent transaction-level calculations. The Excel workbook calculates row-level amounts from the same normalised inputs and matches the SQL results.

What should happen next?

  • Review Fashion returns. Collect return reasons and inspect product-level return rates before proposing a fix.
  • Compare margin and volume. Assess Beauty demand, inventory availability and acquisition costs before recommending more stock or promotion.
  • Review pricing in Electronics. Compare product margins and discount patterns. Revenue leadership alone does not justify a price increase.
  • Improve source quality. Validate dates, quantities, costs and discount ranges at entry. Recover the 18 missing city labels where possible.

These are follow-up questions from a learning dataset. They are not evidence of actions taken or business improvements achieved.

Make the project your own

  1. Open the Excel workbook. Explain each metric and test the city and category selectors.
  2. Run the SQL scripts and compare the eight outputs with the supplied CSV results.
  3. Build the Power BI report from the clean CSV using the report guide and DAX measures.
  4. Add your own question, query and chart. Write a finding that includes a number, its meaning and a limitation.
  5. Save your .pbix file and real report screenshots. Then update the portfolio to reflect what you completed.
